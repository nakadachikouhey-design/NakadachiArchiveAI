# Codex Task Brief: OSAKA FESTIVAL CITY

## Goal
Create a visually compelling proposal deck for Osaka Prefecture and Osaka City that reframes Osaka Fringe as the implementation platform for a broader urban strategy: connecting Umeda / Grand Green Osaka through Midosuji to Namba Hiroba and turning central Osaka into a continuously activated festival city.

This is NOT a subsidy request for Osaka Fringe. The subject is Osaka as a city.

## Core proposition
**イベントをつくる大阪から、祭りが育つ大阪へ。**

Osaka already has high-quality public spaces and private urban assets. The missing layer is not hardware but a durable operating structure that lets citizens, artists, local businesses, railways, developers, cultural venues and public agencies continuously activate those spaces together.

Osaka Fringe should be positioned as the open-access cultural implementation platform within that structure.

## Origin / stakeholder problem statement
This proposal is based on a request from Mr. Nogami, formerly of Dentsu.

Key concerns:

- Namba Hiroba is not a space that belongs only to Nankai Electric Railway; it is a civic urban space and is not yet being used to its full potential.
- Osaka Prefecture / Osaka City previously relied heavily on top-down cultural programming such as Osaka Culture Art Festival, driven by leadership in the Economic Strategy Bureau. With leadership changes, the question is now what sustainable structure should replace one-off event production.
- Osaka did not sufficiently build a structure in which local people, businesses and cultural actors gather and grow an urban festival together over time.
- Grand Green Osaka / Umekita has lawns and new public spaces, but risks functioning mainly as rentable event space rather than as a generator of urban culture.
- Osaka needs a city-scale structure that connects the area from Umekita to Namba Hiroba and generates continuous cultural and visitor activity.
- Osaka Convention & Tourism Bureau is actively supporting Osaka Fringe.
- Kintetsu is cooperative and should be treated as a potential private-sector partner.
- Nankai should be positioned as one participant in a citywide platform, not the sole owner/operator of the Namba concept.

## Strategic framing
Do not write: “Please expand/subsidize Osaka Fringe.”

Write:

> Osaka needs a city operating system that turns existing public spaces, theatres, commercial facilities, hotels, parks, streets and private open spaces into a connected cultural network. Osaka Fringe is an existing implementation platform that can activate that network through open access participation.

### Administrative roles
- Osaka Prefecture / Osaka City: policy, public-space rules, coordination, urban strategy
- Osaka Convention & Tourism Bureau: tourism, international promotion, inbound strategy
- Osaka Fringe: open-access cultural platform / programming and network implementation
- Railways / developers / commercial operators: place, mobility, private urban assets, sponsorship
- Artists / citizens / universities / shops / communities: self-directed participation and content

## Visual direction
This must be image-led, not an ordinary text-heavy government document.

Desired reaction from first 3 slides: **「これや」**

Design principles:

- High-impact full-bleed photography
- Minimal copy per slide
- Strong before/after or world/Osaka visual comparisons
- Sophisticated international cultural-city aesthetic
- Avoid stereotypical Osaka visuals such as takoyaki, Tsutenkaku kitsch, neon collage, comic motifs
- Osaka Fringe brand magenta may be used as an accent, but the deck should represent Osaka as a city rather than look like an Osaka Fringe sales brochure
- Use generous whitespace and strong typography
- Prefer real public-space / festival photography over generic stock imagery
- Every external photo must include a small source credit

## Image strategy
The deck should repeatedly pair:

1. A foreign festival-city precedent showing streets / squares / parks filled with cultural activity
2. A real Osaka Fringe or Osaka urban-space image showing that the same possibility already exists in Osaka

### Priority overseas precedents
Use 3 strong cases only, with one dominant image and one key lesson each:

1. **Edinburgh Festival Fringe**
   - Royal Mile / street events / city becomes stage
   - Point: the festival occupies the city, not only theatres
   - Official source starting point: https://www.edfringe.com/experience/explore-the-fringe/fringe-street-events/

2. **Adelaide Fringe**
   - public realm, park / venue network, citywide participation
   - Point: festival as tourism and economic infrastructure, not just arts programming
   - Official impact source starting point: https://adelaidefringe.com.au/adelaide-fringe-impact

3. **Festival d’Avignon / Avignon Off**
   - streets, courtyards, plazas, whole-city theatrical atmosphere
   - Point: urban identity and festival identity become inseparable
   - Official source starting point: https://festival-avignon.com/

Research and verify all statistics at generation time. Prefer official festival, city, tourism or government sources. Do not copy unsupported numbers from secondary articles.

## Narrative guardrails

- Main subject = Osaka, not Osaka Fringe
- Avoid antagonistic criticism of past Osaka City cultural policy; describe the structural issue neutrally and constructively
- Do not mention an individual bureau director’s retirement in the deck; translate it into the broader policy need for a structure that survives personnel changes
- Avoid language implying that Umekita, Namba Hiroba or private operators are failing; say the next challenge is to move from high-quality space provision to continuous cultural activation
- Keep Nankai, Kintetsu and others in a coalition architecture
- Use the Osaka-Kansai Expo legacy as an opportunity, not as an unsupported claim of official succession
